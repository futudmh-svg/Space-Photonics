Unified Optical Backbone

One coherent photonic backend serving three divergent missions: orbital HPC, hypersonic tracking, and metrology-grade clock transfer. Shared comb. Shared OPA. Swappable front-ends.

🎯 Architecture Thesis

Mechanical gimbals and fixed-point ISL are insufficient for next-generation space infrastructure. A software-defined optical backbone — built around a femtosecond frequency comb, OPA beam steering, and coherent detection — can reconfigure between distributed computing, precision tracking, and clock metrology by swapping front-end optics while keeping the photonic backend identical.

The electronic co-pilot is the same FPGA/DSP chassis running different firmware loads: MPI offload for HPC, Kalman tracking for hypersonic, and a phase-locked stability counter for clock comparison. The photonic backend never changes.

🔧 Shared Photonic Backend

These components are identical across all three missions. They are the "nervous system" of the platform.

1. Optical Frequency Comb (Master Clock)

A single femtosecond pulse train generates thousands of phase-locked tones. One comb seeds all three missions simultaneously.

Comb ToothWavelengthMissionFunction
193 THz1550 nm (C-band)HPC + ISLData modulation, WDM channels, coherent detection LO
281 THz1064 nmTracking / rangingLidar illuminator, Doppler extraction, range gating
385 THz778 nmClock comparisonSr optical lattice clock interrogation, metrology
~1 THz spacing—AllCommon phase reference for coherence across nodes

Why this works: All teeth are phase-locked to the same repetition rate. Doppler shifts, clock drifts, and phase noise are correlated and can be subtracted. Material: TFLN (#1) or InP (#12) for microcomb generation.

2. OPA Switch Fabric

The OPA is not just a beam steerer — it is a reconfigurable optical router. By superimposing multiple phase holograms, a single aperture can form independent beams to 4–8 neighbors simultaneously.

All three missions use the same coherent receiver architecture: signal mixed with a comb tooth (LO), detected on a balanced InGaAs photodiode pair, digitized, and processed by a shared DSP/FPGA chassis.

🔀 Divergent Front-Ends

While the backend is shared, the front-end optics and electronic firmware must diverge for each mission. This is the honest engineering trade-off.

Mission A: Orbital HPC Parallel Synchronization

Vacuum-only Proven heritage

The constellation acts as a single distributed supercomputer. The OPA establishes a reconfigurable optical mesh (torus, hypercube, or all-to-all) based on the workload. White Rabbit-style optical clock transfer achieves picosecond-level synchronization without GPS dependence.

ParameterValueWhy It Matters
Clock stability10⁻¹⁷ @ 1000 sSub-nanosecond MPI barrier sync across 100 nodes
Link latency1–5 ms (LEO crosslink)Deterministic for RDMA
Reconfiguration1–10 µs (OPA)Topology switches between FFT ring and all-reduce tree
Data rate100–400 Gbps per linkCoherent DSP with 16-QAM
Doppler±10 MHz @ 1550 nmLEO orbital mechanics; trivial for DSP
Aperture5–10 cmSmall, light, no AO needed

Key advantage: No atmosphere, no plasma, cooperative beacons. The OPA can run at maximum efficiency without adaptive optics overhead. Flight heritage: LCRD, EDRS, Starlink ISL, TBIRD.

Mission B: Hypersonic Tracking (Atmospheric)

Atmospheric path Uncooperative target Research stage

The same comb tooth (281 THz / 1064 nm) seeds a high-power illuminator. The return is weak, Doppler-shifted by ±10 GHz, and corrupted by atmospheric scintillation. The OPA performs sector scan and fine track; an adaptive optics (AO) deformable mirror corrects wavefront distortion before the coherent mixer.

ParameterValueChallenge
Target speedMach 5–10 (1.7–3.4 km/s)Angular rate 10–40 °/s at 10 km
Doppler shift±10 GHz @ 1064 nmRequires ultra-wideband coherent receiver
Return signal10⁻¹² to 10⁻¹⁰ WShot-noise limited; needs large aperture + AO
Atmospheric turbulencer₀ = 5–15 cmScintillation, beam wander; AO latency 1–10 ms
Plasma sheathnₑ ≈ 10¹⁶–10¹⁸ m⁻³Optical penetrates (ω_laser >> ω_plasma), but emits broadband glow
Aperture30–100 cmMass, thermal, pointing stability
Illuminator power100 W–1 kW (pulsed)Thermal blooming, eye safety, power budget

Critical divergence: This mission requires a separate high-power amplifier (EDFA or Raman) and adaptive optics that the HPC mission does not. The OPA backend is shared, but the front-end telescope and AO loop are mission-specific. The electronic co-pilot runs a Kalman filter with target dynamics model, not MPI offload.

Honest assessment: This is the least mature of the three missions. No deployed system uses all-optical coherent tracking for hypersonic targets. SBIRS/HBTSS use IR thermal emission (passive), not active laser illumination. The optical approach is physically valid but engineering-limited by SNR and atmospheric turbulence.

Mission C: Metrology-Grade Clock Transfer

Vacuum-only NIST demonstrated

Optical lattice clocks (Sr, Yb) achieve 10⁻¹⁸ stability. To compare two clocks in orbit without degrading their performance, the optical phase must be transferred through free space with minimal phase noise. The comb provides a coherent carrier; the OPA maintains pointing; the electronic co-pilot measures and subtracts path-length variations.

ParameterValueWhy It Matters
Clock stability10⁻¹⁸ @ 1 s1 s error per 30 billion years
Transfer stability10⁻¹⁷ @ 1000 sGoal: preserve clock performance across link
Wavelength778 nm (Sr) or 578 nm (Yb)Clock transition wavelengths
Link typeSpace-to-space (vacuum)No atmospheric phase noise
PointingStatic or slow driftOPA locks once; minimal reconfiguration
Power1–5 mWLow power; signal is strong (cooperative beacon)

Key advantage: Cooperative, strong signal, no atmosphere. The hardest problem is not the link but the platform vibration (micro-vibration from reaction wheels, 1–100 Hz, 1–100 µrad). The OPA must track this at kHz rates to preserve phase coherence. Heritage: NIST/JILA free-space optical clock transfer; ESA ACES (mission concept).

⚠️ Honest Limitations

This architecture is physically sound but not yet flight-proven as a unified system. Each piece has heritage; the integration does not.
