📡 EHF vs. Optical Trade Study

Hypersonic Vehicle Communications: 60-100 GHz EHF vs. 1550 nm Free-Space Optical

Executive Summary

For hypersonic vehicle communications, neither EHF nor optical is universally superior. The choice depends on mission phase, atmospheric conditions, and vehicle design constraints. This study evaluates both across 12 criteria with full link budget analysis.

🟢 Optical (1550 nm)

Wins: Plasma penetration, antenna gain, spatial security, spectral availability

Loses: Cloud penetration, pointing complexity, terminal mass

🟡 EHF (60-100 GHz)

Wins: Cloud penetration, mature terminals, all-weather, simpler pointing

Loses: Plasma blackout, limited bandwidth, spectrum allocation

🏆 Recommendation: Hybrid Architecture

Optical primary for high-bandwidth phases (cruise, clear sky). EHF backup for plasma blackout and cloud cover. This is the only architecture that covers all mission phases.

Critical Insight: EHF has 65 dB less antenna gain than optical for the same aperture size. This is fundamental physics — gain scales as (πD/λ)². At 100 GHz, λ = 3 mm. At 1550 nm, λ = 1.55 μm. The ratio is 2000×, or 33 dB. Even with a larger EHF antenna, optical wins by 30+ dB in link margin.

5. Terminal Design Comparison

Optical Terminal

  • Aperture: 5-10 cm OPA or gimbal mirror
  • Mass: 1.5-2.5 kg
  • Power: 5-15 W (laser + TEC)
  • Volume: 15×10×5 cm
  • Window: Sapphire, flush-mounted
  • Pointing: ±15° (OPA) or ±90° (gimbal)
  • Acquisition: Beacon + spiral scan
  • TRL: 5-6 (OPA in space TBD)

EHF Terminal

  • Aperture: 30-60 cm dish or phased array
  • Mass: 4-8 kg
  • Power: 50-200 W (TWT or SSPAs)
  • Volume: 40×30×20 cm
  • Window: Not needed (RF penetrates)
  • Pointing: ±5° (narrow beam)
  • Acquisition: Omnidirectional beacon
  • TRL: 8-9 (proven in space)

6. Contradictions & Confirmation Biases

Confirmation Bias #1: "Optical is always better"

Our architecture assumed optical universally superior. Reality: EHF wins in boost phase and cloud cover. A pure optical architecture would fail 20-30% of the time due to weather. Hybrid is required.

Confirmation Bias #2: "EHF solves plasma blackout"

Some RF advocates claim EHF (100 GHz) avoids blackout. Reality: At peak plasma (n_e = 10¹⁹ m⁻³), f_p = 28 GHz. EHF at 100 GHz has f/f_p = 3.6 — marginal penetration with 10+ dB loss. Not reliable for critical comms.

Contradiction: Optical Terminal Mass

Our architecture claims optical terminal is "lighter." Reality: EHF at 100 GHz needs large dish (5-8 kg). But optical needs precise pointing gimbal or OPA + TEC + laser — also 2-3 kg. Mass advantage is real but smaller than claimed (~2×, not 10×).
