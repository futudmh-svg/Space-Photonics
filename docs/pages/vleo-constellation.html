🎯 2. Two Hypersonic Scenarios

Both scenarios leverage the same constellation infrastructure. The difference is mission mode: passive tracking vs. active two-way control.

🔴 Scenario A: Enemy Hypersonic Tracking

Mission: Detect, track, and triangulate an adversary hypersonic glide vehicle (HGV) using optical sensors on 3+ constellation satellites.

Why Optical Beats RF for Hypersonic Tracking

  • Plasma sheath at Mach 10 has electron density n_e ≈ 10¹⁸ m⁻³
  • Plasma frequency f_p ≈ 9 GHz — blocks S/C/X-band RF completely
  • Optical frequency (193.5 THz) >> f_p — penetrates plasma freely
  • No RF blackout period for optical links

Optical Triangulation Geometry

Triangulation Position Error
σₓ = (r² / B) × σ_α

Where:
r = slant range to target (~300 km)
B = satellite baseline (~500 km cross-plane)
σ_α = angular measurement precision
Centroid Tracking Precision (Quad-Cell)
σ_α = θ_BW / (2√2 × SNR)

θ_BW = λ/D = 15.5 μrad (D = 10 cm, λ = 1550 nm)
SNR = 100 (20 dB) → σ_α ≈ 0.055 μrad = 55 nrad
Resulting Position Accuracy
σₓ = (300,000² / 500,000) × 5.5×10⁻⁸ ≈ 0.01 m = 1 cm

Tracking Loop Requirements

Vehicle speed (Mach 10)3,430 m/s
Max angular rate (satellite view)0.73 °/s
Required tracker bandwidth≥ 100 Hz
OPA phase update rate1 kHz (10× margin)
Tracking modeAuto (no human in loop)

Auto-Tracking State Machine

[ACQUIRE] Satellite scans OPA beam in spiral pattern ↓ Signal detected (plume thermal signature > 500 K) [TRACK] Quad-cell centroid locks, Kalman filter predicts ↓ 2nd satellite acquires, triangulation begins [TRIANGULATE] 3+ satellites, least-squares position fix ↓ Handoff to next satellite pair as geometry degrades [HANDOFF] Predictive handoff using orbital mechanics + AI ↓ Continuous track, 1 cm accuracy, <100 ms latency

🛡️ Scenario B: Friendly Hypersonic Control

Mission: Two-way optical communication with our own hypersonic vehicle for command uplink and telemetry downlink. Multi-beam, multi-face, all-auto.

Why Two-Way Optical is Hard (But Doable)

  • Doppler shift at Mach 10: Δf ≈ 2.2 GHz at 1550 nm
  • Requires coherent receiver with wide capture range
  • Vehicle terminal must survive plasma heating (>2000°C surface)
  • Aperture must be flush-mounted to avoid drag

Doppler Shift Calculation

One-Way Doppler Shift
Δf = f × (v/c) × cos(θ)

f = c/λ = 193.5 THz
v = 3,430 m/s (Mach 10)
θ = 0° (head-on)

Δf = 193.5×10¹² × (3430/3×10⁸) = 2.21 GHz
Doppler Tolerance Assessment
Standard DFB laser linewidth: < 1 MHz
Coherent receiver capture range: typically ±5 GHz
Status: 2.2 GHz is well within capture range
Required: Fast frequency tracking loop (loop BW ~1 kHz)

Link Budget: Satellite → Hypersonic Vehicle

Received Power
P_rx = P_tx + G_tx + G_rx - L_fs - L_atm - L_margin

P_tx = 0 dBW (1 W)
G_tx = 10 log₁₀(η(πD/λ)²) = 103.5 dBi (D=10cm, η=0.55)
G_rx = 10 log₁₀(η(πD/λ)²) = 97.5 dBi (D=5cm, η=0.55)
L_fs = 20 log₁₀(4πR/λ) = 248 dB (R=300km)
L_atm = 0.5 dB (30km altitude, 1550nm)
L_margin = 6 dB

P_rx = 0 + 103.5 + 97.5 - 248 - 0.5 - 6 = -53.5 dBW = -23.5 dBm
Link Margin
PPLN receiver sensitivity @ 10 Gbps: ~-60 dBm
Received power: -23.5 dBm
Margin: 36.5 dB — more than adequate

Multi-Beam Multi-Face Operation

📐 4. Complete System Mathematics

4.1 Plasma Physics — Why Optical Wins

Plasma Frequency
ω_p = √(n_e × e² / (m_e × ε₀))

f_p = ω_p / 2π = (1/2π) × √(n_e × e² / (m_e × ε₀))

For n_e = 10¹⁸ m⁻³, e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C, m_e = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg, ε₀ = 8.85×10⁻¹² F/m:

f_p = (1/2π) × √(10¹⁸ × (1.6×10⁻¹⁹)² / (9.11×10⁻³¹ × 8.85×10⁻¹²))
f_p = (1/2π) × √(2.56×10⁻²⁰ / 8.06×10⁻⁴²)
f_p = (1/2π) × √(3.18×10²¹)
f_p = (1/2π) × 5.64×10¹⁰ = 8.97 GHz ≈ 9 GHz

Conventional RF (S-band 2 GHz, C-band 4 GHz, X-band 8 GHz) is below plasma cutoff → total reflection. Optical at 193.5 THz is 21,000× above cutoff → full penetration.

4.2 Doppler Shift — Full Derivation

Relativistic Doppler (for v << c)
f' = f × (1 + v_r/c) / √(1 - v²/c²) ≈ f × (1 + v_r/c) [non-relativistic approx]

Δf = f × v_r / c

For Mach 10: v = 3430 m/s, λ = 1550 nm → f = 193.5 THz
Δf = 193.5×10¹² × 3430 / 3×10⁸ = 2.21×10⁹ Hz = 2.21 GHz

Round-trip (radar): Δf_round = 2 × Δf = 4.42 GHz

4.3 Link Budget — Full Calculation

Free Space Loss
L_fs = (4πR/λ)²
L_fs(dB) = 20 log₁₀(4πR/λ) = 20 log₁₀(4π × 3×10⁵ / 1.55×10⁻⁶)
L_fs(dB) = 20 log₁₀(2.43×10¹²) = 247.7 dB
Antenna Gain (circular aperture, efficiency η = 0.55)
G = η × (πD/λ)²
G_tx = 0.55 × (π × 0.1 / 1.55×10⁻⁶)² = 0.55 × 4.11×10¹⁰ = 2.26×10¹⁰ → 103.5 dBi
G_rx = 0.55 × (π × 0.05 / 1.55×10⁻⁶)² = 0.55 × 1.03×10¹⁰ = 5.65×10⁹ → 97.5 dBi
Received Power
P_rx = P_tx + G_tx + G_rx - L_fs - L_atm - L_point - L_margin

= 0 + 103.5 + 97.5 - 247.7 - 0.5 - 3.0 - 6.0
= -56.2 dBW = -26.2 dBm

PPLN receiver sensitivity @ 10 Gbps, BER 10⁻⁹: -60 dBm
Link margin: 33.8 dB

4.4 Turbulence at 30 km Altitude

Fried Parameter r₀ (slanted path from space to 30km)
r₀ = [0.423 × k² × ∫ Cn²(z) dz]^{-3/5}

k = 2π/λ = 4.05×10⁶ m⁻¹
Cn² at 30km ≈ 10⁻¹⁷ m^{-2/3}
Path length: 30 km
∫ Cn² dz ≈ 10⁻¹⁷ × 3×10⁴ = 3×10⁻¹³

r₀ = [0.423 × (4.05×10⁶)² × 3×10⁻¹³]^{-3/5}
r₀ = [0.423 × 1.64×10¹³ × 3×10⁻¹³]^{-3/5}
r₀ = [2.08]^{-3/5} = 0.59 m = 59 cm

For a 5 cm receiver aperture: D/r₀ = 0.085 << 1 → turbulence has minimal effect. For a 10 cm transmitter: D/r₀ = 0.17 → slight beam spreading (~0.2 μrad), negligible for this application.

4.5 OPA Multi-Beam Power Splitting

Power per Beam (N simultaneous beams)
P_beam = P_total / N
For N = 4 beams: P_beam = P_total / 4 → -6 dB per beam

Received power per beam: -26.2 dBm - 6 dB = -32.2 dBm
Still 27.8 dB above sensitivity → viable

4.6 Constellation Orbital Mechanics

Orbital Period (circular orbit, h = 300 km)
T = 2π × √((R_earth + h)³ / μ)

R_earth = 6371 km, h = 300 km, μ = 3.986×10¹⁴ m³/s²
T = 2π × √((6.671×10⁶)³ / 3.986×10¹⁴)
T = 2π × √(2.97×10²⁰ / 3.986×10¹⁴)
T = 2π × √(7.45×10⁵) = 2π × 863 = 5,425 s ≈ 90.4 min
Orbital Velocity
v = √(μ / (R_earth + h)) = √(3.986×10¹⁴ / 6.671×10⁶) = 7,730 m/s
Maximum View Time per Satellite (5° min elevation)
η = arcsin(R_earth / (R_earth + h) × cos(5°)) = arcsin(0.956 × 0.996) = arcsin(0.952) = 72.3°
View angle = 2 × (90° - 72.3°) = 35.4°
View time = T × (35.4° / 360°) = 5425 × 0.098 = 532 s ≈ 8.9 min

🟡 C2: 400 Gbps Per-Link vs. State of Art

Location: architecture.html, link-budget.html

Claim: 400 Gbps per OISL link

Reality: NASA LCRD: 1.2 Gbps. Mynaric CONDOR: 100 Gbps (announced, not flown). No 400 Gbps OISL demonstrated.

Cross-reference: NASA NTRS 20010049375; Mynaric public specs 2024.

Assessment: ⚠️ ASPIRATIONAL — 4-20× ahead of current flight hardware. Achievable with advanced modulation but not yet demonstrated.

🟡 C3: All-Optical Mesh — No O-E-O Conversion

Location: architecture.html

Claim: "All-optical routing with no O-E-O conversion"

Reality: OPA beam steering requires electronic phase control. Packet routing decisions require buffering/forwarding logic. True all-optical switching (MZI-based) at TRL 4-5.

Cross-reference: Keysight (2025) — phased array multi-beam requires digital beamforming. EDRS operational OISL uses electronic routing.

Assessment: ⚠️ OVERSIMPLIFIED — Control plane is electronic. Data plane can be all-optical with MZI switches.

🔴 C4: Hypersonic Vehicle Terminal Not Designed

Location: Entire app

Issue: Extensive satellite-side design (OPA, PPLN) but zero vehicle terminal design. The hypersonic vehicle needs: (a) optical window surviving >2000°C, (b) aperture not creating drag, (c) tracking through plasma.

Cross-reference: NASA NTRS 20100008938 — blackout mitigation studies. Navy SBIR N202-107 (2020) — RF communication through plasma sheath.

Assessment: 🔴 CRITICAL GAP — Vehicle terminal is the other half of the link. Needs flush-mounted sapphire window + miniaturized OPA or gimbal.

🟡 C5: Atmospheric Loss Underestimated for Ground Links

Location: link-budget.html

Claim: ~3 dB atmospheric loss

Reality: For satellite-to-ground through thick clouds, attenuation can exceed 30 dB. No weather diversity or cloud-free line-of-sight analysis included.

Cross-reference: ESA EDRS experience — optical ground stations require clear sky. Availability ~80-90% at good sites.

Assessment: ⚠️ INCOMPLETE — Needs cloud statistics and ground station diversity for availability >99%.

🟡 C6: VLEO Drag Not Quantified

Location: vleo-constellation.html

Issue: 300 km orbit requires continuous thrust for station-keeping. No propulsion budget or drag analysis.

Cross-reference: SpaceX Starlink orbits at 550 km (reduced drag). GOCE flew at 260 km with ion propulsion (2013-2013).

Assessment: ⚠️ MISSING — Add drag analysis. Consider 400-500 km orbit for longer lifetime.

🟢 C7: Optical Plasma Penetration — VALIDATED

Claim: Optical penetrates hypersonic plasma sheath

Verification: f_p ≈ 9 GHz for typical plasma. Optical at 193.5 THz is 21,500× above cutoff. Multiple sources confirm (NASA, Notre Dame, Xidian University 2024).

Assessment: ✅ VALIDATED — This is a genuine advantage of optical over RF for hypersonic applications.

🟢 C8: PPLN Sensitivity — PLAUSIBLE

Claim: −65/−70 dBm detection with PPLN cascaded χ⁽²⁾

Verification: Stanford 2023 demonstrated −60 dBm @ 10 Gbps with PPLN. −70 dBm is achievable with: (a) longer crystal, (b) lower temperature, (c) higher pump power.

Assessment: ✅ PLAUSIBLE — Within reach of demonstrated technology with marginal improvements.
